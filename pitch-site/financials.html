Section 07

What Could Go Wrong

And Why We've Thought Through It

Honest critical analysis. No startup is risk-free. These are the real risks — and the mitigations we've already built or planned.

Risk 1 — Platform DOM Fragility

Impact: High Probability: Medium

Amazon, Twitter, and YouTube update their HTML regularly. Extension adapters break when DOM selectors change. User experience degrades until adapters are patched. At scale, even a 24-hour breakage window creates churn risk.

Mitigation
Automated selector health monitoring (SelectorHealth.ts already built and in production). Fast-update CI/CD pipeline reduces patch window to under 2 hours. GenericAdapter fallback keeps core functionality alive even when site-specific adapters break. Monitoring alerts fire before users notice.

Risk 2 — Big Tech Enters the Space

Impact: High Probability: Low–Medium

Google ships "interest graph as a service." OpenAI ships persistent preference memory across all apps. A single hyperscaler announcement creates a perception problem, even before a viable product exists.

Mitigation
18–24 month first-mover window is the core thesis. Compliance certifications (HIPAA/SOC2) take 12–18 months for any competitor to replicate. Switching costs from graph data create stickiness that survives a competitive announcement. Decentralized architecture is structurally impossible for Google to replicate — it requires centralized data control. The user-ownership model is politically untenable for Big Tech to copy given their regulatory environment.

Risk 3 — Cold Start Problem

Impact: Medium Probability: High

The first 3 sessions feel basic. New users don't yet have enough graph data to experience the product's full value. They churn before the graph is rich enough to deliver the "aha" moment. This is the most likely failure mode for consumer acquisition.

Mitigation
Import from Spotify, Netflix, YouTube, and Reddit data takeouts on Day 1 — graph is pre-seeded before the user even browses. Onboarding questionnaire seeds initial interest nodes immediately. Vertical-calibrated defaults for SDK customers (healthcare users get healthcare-relevant defaults). "Quick wins" — visible preference cards and agent actions — are designed into the first-session flow to demonstrate value before the graph is rich.

Risk 4 — Enterprise Sales Cycle Length

Impact: Medium Probability: Medium

HIPAA-regulated enterprise customers take 6–9 months to close. Security reviews, legal review, procurement cycles, and budget approval all extend the sales cycle. Cash flow risk if the pipeline is enterprise-heavy in Year 1.

Mitigation
Self-serve Growth tier ($499/mo) creates revenue without enterprise sales cycles — developers can start immediately with a credit card. Pantory as a reference customer shortens healthcare deal cycles by an estimated 60% (peer reference dramatically reduces security review burden). Upfront annual contracts reduce duration risk once deals close. Pipeline is intentionally seeded with mid-market accounts alongside enterprise targets.

Risk 5 — SDK Commoditization

Impact: Medium Probability: Low

OpenAI or Anthropic add preference graph features to their core APIs in 2027. Developers ask: "Why buy the RecoMe SDK when the LLM API already has memory?" The SDK's differentiation erodes at the commodity layer.

Mitigation
By 2027, RecoMe SDK customers have 2 years of accumulated, structured graph data that doesn't migrate to a new system. Compliance certifications (HIPAA/SOC2) are in place — LLM providers cannot easily match regulated-vertical requirements. The module marketplace will have 100+ domain-specific modules representing expert-contributed knowledge. The product becomes infrastructure — not a feature someone would replace without significant cost and disruption.

Risk 6 — Consumer Retention

Impact: High Probability: Medium

Extension and app users churn after initial interest fades. Consumer SaaS churn is notoriously high. The product is novel but novelty doesn't sustain subscriptions. Month 3–6 is the churn danger window.

Mitigation
The graph gets more valuable over time — compounding value model means the longer a user stays, the more they'd lose by leaving (switching cost builds automatically). ShopperAgent price alerts create habit-forming push notifications that bring users back daily. Weekly digest emails from ScoutAgent create regular touchpoints even for passive users. Team plans lock in via shared graph utility — churning affects colleagues, not just oneself. Annual subscription pricing reduces payment churn.
