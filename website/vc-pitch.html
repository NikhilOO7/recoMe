The Problem
Every AI App Built in 2024–2025
Is Stateless. All of Them.

The LLM revolution created an entire category of AI-native applications. They have one thing in common: they know nothing about you by the time you leave.

🔄

The Reset Problem

Every session starts from zero. A medical report app re-explains what CA 19-9 means to a patient who has uploaded 6 reports. A legal tool re-orients to the same contract context every visit. The user's history is lost the moment they close the tab.

⚖️

The Equal-Weight Problem

LLMs treat all content with equal weight. A new hepatic lesion (urgent) and stable background inflammation (irrelevant) get the same explanation priority. The patient doesn't know what to call their doctor about — and neither does the app.

🏗️

The Build-It-Yourself Problem

Companies that recognize this problem face a choice: spend 12 months and $500K building a preference graph, ranking system, and memory layer from scratch — or ship a worse product. 99% of companies ship the worse product.

The cost of this problem: Companies lose 40–60% of user retention at the second session because the product feels like it learned nothing. Every return visit is a first visit. In high-stakes domains — healthcare, legal, finance — this isn't just a product problem. It's a trust problem.

The Core Insight
"Personalization infrastructure is the missing layer of the AI stack. Every company is reinventing it. Nobody is selling it."

The AI stack in 2026 has models (OpenAI, Anthropic), vector databases (Pinecone, Weaviate), and LLM frameworks (LangChain, LlamaIndex). What doesn't exist is a layer that builds and maintains a model of the individual user and uses it to rank what they should see next.

What exists

The Model Layer

GPT-5, Claude 4, Gemini 2. Brilliant at understanding content. Know nothing about who is reading it.

What exists

The Retrieval Layer

Pinecone, Weaviate, RAG pipelines. Find similar content. Still no model of the user. Content-to-content, not user-to-content.

The Gap — We Fill This

The User Intelligence Layer

Per-user interest graph + ranked scoring + cross-session memory. This doesn't exist as a product yet.

Product
What Each Module Does —
Technical Depth

Three modules that work as a system. Each one solves a specific gap in the AI stack. Together they produce capabilities that don't exist in any current product.

Module What It Does What It Enables (That Nothing Else Does)
🧠 Interest Graph Stores per-user entity relationships as typed, weighted edges. Entities: conditions, topics, terms, concepts. Relations: interested_in, confused_by, tracks, has_condition. Weights decay over time, spike on interaction. User-to-content matching (not item-to-item). Works from session 1. Longitudinal delta detection. "What changed for this user since their last interaction with this content type."
⚖️ OpportunityRanker Scores each content item across 4 signals: Interest Match (35%), Benefit Clarity (25%), Timeliness (20%), Novelty (20%). Applies a relevance threshold. Returns ranked list with scores and source signals. Critical findings always surface first regardless of document order. Stable background noise filtered out. Prioritization adapts to this user's specific history — not a global model.
🗂️ Memory Pack Short-term (session) and mid-term (cross-session) memory cards per user, organized by topic bucket. Tracks what terms were explained, what sections re-read, what questions asked. Prevents re-explaining concepts the user already understands. Reduces LLM token consumption by 30–50% on repeat users. Makes session 5 feel dramatically different from session 1.
🔍 Profile Extractor Modular registry pattern. Each specialty module (oncology, contract law, GAAP financials) knows its document structure and outputs a structured finding object. All modules feed the same graph. One SDK across infinite domains. Customer integrates once. Switches specialty modules based on their content type. Third parties can build and sell modules in the marketplace.
